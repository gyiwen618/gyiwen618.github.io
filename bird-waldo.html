Bird-Inspired Waldo System

Yiwen Gao · MEAM 5100 Design of Mechatronic Systems · University of Pennsylvania · October 2026

A handheld input mechanism maps wing and tail movements to a servo-driven bird, combining mechanical design, sensor calibration, embedded programming, and hardware integration.

Mechanism and Fabrication

The input device uses three potentiometers to sense left-wing, right-wing, and tail motion. A gear mechanism couples the two wing inputs so they move symmetrically, giving the system two independent motions: wing flapping and tail rotation. On the output side, three SG90 servos actuate the two wings and tail. I designed the assemblies in CAD and laser-cut the wings and body plates from 1/8-inch MDF.

Sensing and Control

I characterized the potentiometers across their 270° travel and mapped their ADC readings to servo commands on an ItsyBitsy microcontroller. The servos receive 50 Hz PWM signals. To support the motor current, I powered them from an external 5 V supply and connected its ground to the controller ground for a common signal reference.

Engineering Analysis

Using the wing mass and center of mass from the CAD model, I checked the servo against the maximum static gravitational torque. After an estimated 20% mechanical loss and a safety factor of three, the available torque was about 5.55 times the requirement. I also estimated the worst-case current draw to plan the power supply.
